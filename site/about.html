About

Public filings, read for commercial real estate borrowers

LenderMapper is a free tool for commercial real estate borrowers. Enter a property address and it lists the banks and credit unions nearby, ranked by how likely they are to lend on commercial real estate, using only the call reports every insured lender files each quarter.

Call reports through the latest published quarter.

What it shows

Two lists for every address. The first holds lenders whose main office is in the property's county. The second holds lenders based elsewhere in the same state with a branch in the county, or within a distance you choose. Each list is ordered by a national score, and every lender gets a grade from A+ to D.

The grades sort lenders; they are not approval odds. The score is a simple, open ranking of four public figures and cannot say whether a given lender will approve a given loan.

Nothing here is a recommendation, an offer of credit, or a statement by any lender. Sponsored placements are always labelled and never change a score, grade or position in the lists.

How the score works

Four measures count equally: loan to deposit (lower is better), CRE lending capacity in dollars (higher is better), the capital ratio (higher is better) and funding cost (lower is better). Each lender is ranked on each measure against every bank and credit union in the country and earns 4 points for the best quarter, then 3, 2 and 1. The score is the total, from 4 to 16.

Banks and credit unions follow different rules for capital and for how much commercial real estate they can hold, so tier 1 leverage and bank lending capacity are ranked among banks only, and the net worth ratio and credit-union lending capacity among credit unions only. Low-income-designated credit unions have no business-loan cap and earn the full 4 points for capacity.

Grades: A+ for all 16 points, then A, B, C and D for the four national quarters of the score. On the results page, a green box marks any headline figure in the top third of the lenders in that search.

Where the data comes from

  • FDIC BankFind Suite: bank institutions, branch locations, quarterly call-report financials and the annual Summary of Deposits.
  • NCUA: Form 5300 call-report data and credit-union branch files.
  • FRED: fed funds, SOFR, Treasury yields and the Senior Loan Officer Survey, used in the research behind the weights.
  • U.S. Census Bureau geocoder: credit-union office coordinates.
  • OpenStreetMap, Photon and OpenFreeMap: address lookup and map tiles.

Figures are as reported by each institution, with no adjustments. Bank and credit-union definitions sometimes differ; each difference is footnoted in the definitions below.

How often it updates

Call reports are published roughly 60 days after each quarter ends; branch deposits once a year, for June 30, published in late September. The site rebuilds on the 1st and 15th of each month and picks up new quarters automatically.
