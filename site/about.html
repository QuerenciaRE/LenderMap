About

Public filings, read for commercial real estate borrowers

LenderMapper is a free tool for commercial real estate borrowers. Enter a property address and it lists the banks and credit unions nearby, ranked by how likely they are to lend on commercial real estate, using only the call reports every insured lender files each quarter.

Call reports through the latest published quarter.

What it shows

Two lists for every address. The first holds lenders whose main office is in the property's county. The second holds lenders based elsewhere in the same state with a branch in the county, or within a distance you choose. Each list is ordered by a likelihood-to-lend score, and every lender gets a tier from A to E.

The tiers sort lenders; they are not approval odds. The model behind the score cannot say how much a given lender will lend, but it reliably separates lenders that grow their commercial real estate books from those that do not.

Nothing here is a recommendation, an offer of credit, or a statement by any lender. Sponsored placements are always labelled and never change a score, tier or position in the lists.

How the score works

Six parts, each a national percentile from 0 to 100, weighted 25/25/15/15/10/10 by default: CRE momentum, CRE franchise, deposit growth, asset quality, capital and margin. Penalties apply where lending historically slowed: banks above 400% of capital in investment real estate, construction above 150%, and credit unions near their business-loan cap.

The weights come from a test on 381,507 lender-quarters from 2016 to 2026. Trained through 2022 and tested on 2023 to 2025, the ranking had a rank correlation of 0.37 for banks and 0.33 for credit unions with the next year's CRE growth. The top tenth of banks grew CRE by 4.28 points of assets; the bottom tenth by 0.09.

Where the data comes from

  • FDIC BankFind Suite: bank institutions, branch locations, quarterly call-report financials and the annual Summary of Deposits.
  • NCUA: Form 5300 call-report data and credit-union branch files.
  • FRED: fed funds, SOFR, Treasury yields and the Senior Loan Officer Survey, used in the research behind the weights.
  • U.S. Census Bureau geocoder: credit-union office coordinates.
  • OpenStreetMap, Photon and OpenFreeMap: address lookup and map tiles.

Figures are as reported by each institution, with no adjustments. Bank and credit-union definitions sometimes differ; each difference is footnoted in the definitions below.

How often it updates

Call reports are published roughly 60 days after each quarter ends; branch deposits once a year, for June 30, published in late September. The site rebuilds on the 1st and 15th of each month and picks up new quarters automatically.
